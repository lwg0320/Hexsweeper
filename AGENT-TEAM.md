# Agent-team log

I started with a Pool Shark reconstruction, then changed direction to Hexsweeper when I wanted a small game I could finish and test. I chose a six-neighbor hex grid, three map sizes, a safe first reveal, and the basic reveal/flag actions. I later added a Claude Haiku 5.5 player beside the deterministic solver, with prompt caching, an explicit cost notice, and one retry before a labeled legal fallback.

For the in-game comparison, I chose the constraint solver as the baseline because random moves would be a weak Minesweeper competitor. The Claude player gets public observations and legal actions, without repository tools or the mine layout. In the five-seed, two-trial-per-seed comparison, the solver won all 10 episodes and both Claude configurations lost all 10. I report that result as limited to this suite in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md).

I watched a friend play all three sizes without helping. They hesitated after an early reveal gave little information and thought the game was relatively easy. I kept the rules unchanged; I would clarify that a safe first reveal can show only a clue. The notes are in [PLAYTEST-NOTES.md](PLAYTEST-NOTES.md).
